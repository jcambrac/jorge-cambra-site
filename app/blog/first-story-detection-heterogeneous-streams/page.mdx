# First Story Detection over Heterogeneous Streams

## Introduction 

First Story Detection (FSD) is the task of identyfying the first document, news article, or social media post
that reports a newly emerging event from a continuous, real-time stream of data.

Put simply: given documents arriving over time, flag the ones about something nobody has discussed before.

It was formalized in the late 1990s on newswire, as part of the Topic Detection and Tracking program, and later revived for Twitter.

The core idea is to compare each new document to everything seen so far. If its nearest neighbor is far enough away, call it new.

## Why is this useful

Imagine you are trying to warn people about global catastrophes before they happen: a new pathogen, a military escalation, a dangerous jump in AI capabilities. That is what forecasting and early-warning organizations like Sentinel try to do. In that line of work, it is clearly a good thing to be aware of everything new that is going on, as early as possible.

The catch is that new things rarely show up first where you would look for them. A local outbreak might surface in a regional subreddit days before a wire service picks it up. A leak or a rumor might appear on 4chan, get dismissed, and later turn out to be real. Mainstream news arrives later, but it is cleaner and easier to trust.

Each source on its own gives a partial picture. Forums and imageboards are early but noisy; news is reliable but late. Watching them together lets you catch a story at its first appearance, then confirm it as it spreads to more credible sources.
